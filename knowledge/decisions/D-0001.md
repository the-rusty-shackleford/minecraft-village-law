# D-0001 — Skyrim's guards over Thief's

Accepted 2026-10-03, built 2026-10-04. Rusty passed on the ask: a guard who sees a crime runs
over and offers *pay the fine or leave for two days* instead of attacking, on Thief's reputation.
His calls: a new mod named **Village Law** (not "Village Guards", which reads like Guard
Villagers), and paying the fine on the spot also clears the crime from the villagers' memory.

## What the code it sits on does

These were checked in the decompiled jars the box runs: Thief 1.2.4 and Guard Villagers 2.4.11
(sha1 5168ed3f). The box's configs were read on 2026-10-04.

- Thief's `Crime.commit` finds witnesses and runs each witness's reaction handler. It then fires
  `CrimeCommitedEvent` with the witness list. Its guard handler sets a guard or golem on the
  criminal when `Crime.shouldGuardsAttack` says so (threshold MEDIUM on the box). It asks once
  per guard, before the event.
- Guard Villagers' `DefendVillageGuardGoal`, and its mixin into the golem's
  `DefendVillageTargetGoal`, target any player a nearby villager rates at −100 or below (the
  box's value). A witnessed heavy crime costs each witness −150: 20 major negative at −5, and 50
  minor at −1. So switching off Thief's handler alone would still leave guards and golems
  attacking on reputation.
- Every `Mob.setTarget` fires NeoForge's cancellable `LivingChangeTargetEvent`.
- Guards keep gossip of their own, picked up from villagers, and attack on it.
- A mob's flat-ground speed is `0.98 (base × modifier)² / (1 − 0.546)`: the move control sets
  both its speed and its forward input to that product. A sprinting player's is
  `0.98 × 0.13 / 0.454` = 0.2806 blocks a tick. Guard Villagers' base is 0.5 on the box and its
  melee goal's modifier 0.8, which runs 23% faster than a sprint. Bases are saved with each mob.

## The rule

- **A case** opens for (player, village) when a crime at or over Thief's guard threshold, by a
  player, in a village Village Deed's protocol knows, is seen by an officer (`villagelaw:officers`).
  Fines go by grade (3, 8, 15), and further crimes add up. A crime only villagers saw stays
  Thief's alone.
- **Thief's attack** is switched off by one mixin at the head of `shouldGuardsAttack`, only where
  the law takes the crime. It asks the same question the event will: a player, in a village,
  seen by an officer. The answer is kept for the tick, because Thief asks once per guard.
- **The summons:** the nearest officer who saw the crime runs at its chase speed and serves the
  summons within three blocks. The screen offers Pay or Leave. Escape, walking away, or any other
  way of closing it counts as Leave.
- **Pay** takes the fine through Carried (Village Deed's payment rule: emeralds first, then
  blocks, with change), closes the case, and forgives the payer: their major and minor negative
  gossip is removed from every villager and guard in the vicinity, and nobody else's.
- **Leave** gives 60 seconds to get out of the vicinity (bounds + 32). Out in time: banished for
  two days, owing the fine. Inside at 0:00: hostile until the player leaves or dies, and the
  banishment runs from then. Banished and back early: attacked while inside. After the two days:
  a debt, payable to any officer.
- **The hold-back:** while a case is open and not hostile, the village's guards and golems may
  not target the player. That is a `LivingChangeTargetEvent` cancel, so it covers Guard
  Villagers' −100 rule, the golem's defence and anything else. It lifts for five seconds after
  the player hurts a villager, an officer or a golem: a fight the player starts is a fight.
- **Guard speed:** each officer's movement-speed base is set as it joins a level, to the value
  whose chase equals `chase_speed` (default 1.0) times a sprint: 0.4507.
- **The state machine** is pure (`domain/Case`). The clock is passed in, so the GameTests look at
  a player two days later without waiting. `Law.move` is the one place a state change is acted
  on.

## Also decided while building

- **A WANTED or SUMMONED player who walks out of the vicinity is banished**, as if they had
  chosen Leave. Walking away from the summons is leaving it.
- **A summons left on screen at logout lapses back to WANTED**, so an officer comes again when
  the player returns. A crash or a disconnect is not taken as an answer.
- **A banished player who comes back and leaves again is stood down**: the enforcers attack a
  banished player only while the player is inside. Without this they would chase the player out
  of the village until they lost track.
- **Standing down clears anger, not only the target.** A golem or guard keeps a persistent grudge
  that would re-target the player the next tick.
- **The screen dims the world instead of blurring it, and sits below the crosshair**, so the
  guard who stopped the player stands in view (judged in the booth photographs).
- **`CaseSettledEvent`** (PAID, or FLED when a banishment begins) is the extension point for
  Serfdom's capture remedy.

## Rejected

- Replacing Thief's guard handler and nothing else: Guard Villagers' reputation rule would still
  attack after a heavy crime (above).
- Editing Guard Villagers' speed config: bases are saved with each guard, so only guards spawned
  later would slow.
